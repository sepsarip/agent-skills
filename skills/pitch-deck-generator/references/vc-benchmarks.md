# Venture Capital Pitch Deck Benchmarks & Red Flags
*Synthesized from Sequoia Capital, Y Combinator (YC), and DocSend Empirical Pitch Deck Research.*

## 1. The Investor Skim Test Reality
* Investors spend an average of **2 minutes and 40 seconds** viewing a pitch deck (DocSend).
* Investors skim **Slide Headlines first** before reading bullet points. Therefore, every slide heading MUST be an **Action Title** stating the conclusion, never a generic label.
  * ❌ Bad: `## Problem`
  * ✅ Good: `## Problem: Mid-Market Logistics Lose 14% Margin to Manual Freight Auditing`

---

## 2. Standard 12-Slide Narrative Arc

1. **Slide 1 — Title & Company Purpose**: Articulate the company's position in 1 declarative sentence (*"We help [ICP] achieve [Outcome] by [Mechanism]"*) with zero buzzwords.
2. **Slide 2 — Problem (Hair-on-Fire Pain)**: Quantify the economic/time loss suffered by a specific ICP and expose why the current *status quo* fails. *(Exception: If ARR ≥ $1M, move Slide 7 Traction here).*
3. **Slide 3 — Solution (1:1 Mirroring)**: Directly mirror the 3 pain points on Slide 2 with 3 concrete outcomes. Focus on measurable benefits, not feature lists.
4. **Slide 4 — Why Now? (Catalyst)**: Identify the recent (6–18 months) technological, regulatory, or behavioral shift that makes this business viable *today* and impossible 3 years ago.
5. **Slide 5 — Market Size (Bottoms-Up Math)**: Quantify TAM, SAM, and SOM using bottoms-up unit math (`Number of Identified Customers × Annual Contract Value`).
6. **Slide 6 — Product & Workflow**: Show how the product works in 3 clean steps (`Input ➡️ Core Engine ➡️ Output`) using a simple `flowchart LR` diagram or visual workflow.
7. **Slide 7 — Execution Traction**: Present hard momentum signals (ARR/MRR, MoM growth, retention cohorts, LOIs, waitlist velocity, or technical benchmarks).
8. **Slide 8 — Business Model & Unit Economics**: Explain pricing tiers, revenue model, Gross Margin, `LTV:CAC` ratio, and CAC Payback Period.
9. **Slide 9 — Competition & Defensible Moat**: Map competitors (including the *Status Quo*) across key criteria and articulate a real moat (*Data Flywheel, Workflow Lock-In, Network Effect, or Switching Cost*).
10. **Slide 10 — Team (Founder-Market Fit)**: Highlight shipping credentials, domain expertise, and why this team is uniquely equipped to win.
11. **Slide 11 — 3-Year Financial Projections**: Rational bottoms-up forecast showing Customers, ARR, Gross Margin, Burn Rate, and Key Revenue Drivers.
12. **Slide 12 — The Ask & 18-Month Milestones**: Specify capital raised, percentage use of funds (R&D, GTM, Ops), and specific operational milestones achieved within 18–24 months.

## 3. The 8 Fatal VC Red Flags (Strictly Forbidden)

1. **Claiming "No Competitors"**: Ignoring the status quo (Excel, WhatsApp, manual labor) signals market naivety.
2. **Top-Down Market Hallucinations**: Quoting generic consulting reports (*"We only need 1% of a $50B market"*) destroys credibility instantly.
3. **Generic "Proprietary AI" Moat Claims**: Foundation model wrappers are easily commoditized; moats must come from private datasets, workflow integration, or distribution.
4. **The Ask Without Milestones**: Asking for $1M without specifying what ARR or user milestone that capital unlocks in 18 months.
5. **Opening with Disclaimers or SWOT**: Never put legal disclaimers or SWOT matrices in the pitch deck.
6. **Text Walls (>45 Words per Slide)**: Paragraphs of prose cause immediate investor drop-off.
7. **Fantasy Hockey-Stick Financials**: Projecting $0 to $100M ARR in 3 years without unit-driver math (`Customers × ACV`).
8. **Burying Real Traction**: If a startup has real revenue or explosive growth, highlight it immediately rather than hiding it at the end.
